ADVANCED DEVICES · INTERMEDIATE

Mobile application testing

Build a practical mobile test matrix around devices, operating systems, connectivity, permissions, app lifecycle, and releases instead of trying every possible combination.

SELF-PACED5 LESSONSHANDS-ON PRACTICE

What you will learn

  • Choose representative device and OS coverage from user and risk data.
  • Test interruptions, permissions, connectivity, and app lifecycle.
  • Check touch, screen adaptation, and device-specific behavior.
  • Separate product defects from device and environment issues.

Course lessons

1. Select a representative device matrix

Use product analytics, supported-device policy, and risk to select coverage. Include important OS versions, screen sizes, and capabilities rather than an arbitrary long list. Combine real devices and emulators where useful. Record what is covered and why gaps remain.

2. Test lifecycle and interruption

Exercise install, first launch, background and resume, force close, upgrade, and uninstall where applicable. Interrupt a task with a call, notification, or permission prompt. Verify progress is retained or recovered according to product behavior and private content is not exposed unexpectedly.

3. Vary network and permissions

Test reliable, slow, intermittent, and unavailable connections in a controlled environment. Check loading, retry, timeout, and offline messaging. Grant, deny, and later revoke permissions such as camera or location. Confirm denial does not crash or block unrelated tasks.

4. Review touch and platform behavior

Check orientation, safe areas, keyboard appearance, scrolling, back navigation, deep links, and system bars on representative devices. Verify touch targets are operable and text remains usable when system font size changes. Compare supported platforms without assuming they must behave identically.

5. Practice: test a photo upload flow

On two representative devices, upload a photo from camera and gallery. Deny camera access, revoke it in settings, switch networks during upload, background the app, and resume. Record device, OS, app build, steps, and evidence. Group findings by user impact and likely cause.

Takeaway

Use a focused, documented approach. Choose checks based on risk, preserve useful evidence, and make the next quality decision clear.
